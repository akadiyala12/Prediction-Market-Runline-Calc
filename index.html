How the split and fees work

Wager 1 is the wider market (it also wins when the result lands exactly on the number). Wager 2 is the narrower one. To risk R, put R·p on wager 1 and R·(1−p) on wager 2. Landing on the number pays exactly R back, a push. The created line is priced at q / (q + 1 − p).

Both platforms charge takers rate × contracts × price × (1 − price). Polymarket takes the fee out of the shares you receive, which is why your payout came in under the full amount. That works out to an effective price of p / (1 − rate·(1−p)). Kalshi adds the fee to your cost, an effective price of p·(1 + rate·(1−p)), and rounds each order's fee up to the cent. The calculator uses these effective prices so the push still breaks even after fees. Resting limit orders that get filled (maker orders) usually pay no fee on either platform.
